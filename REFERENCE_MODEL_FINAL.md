# Reinforcement Learning-Based Adaptive Pilot and Power Allocation for Joint Synchronization and Sensing in OFDM-ISAC Systems

## Abstract
This paper proposes a reinforcement learning-based adaptive pilot and power allocation framework for joint synchronization and sensing in OFDM-based Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC) systems. Unlike conventional systems where pilot density and power allocation are fixed, the proposed framework dynamically adapts these parameters based on channel conditions and synchronization quality. A mathematical system model incorporating time-varying carrier frequency offset (CFO), multipath fading, and additive noise is developed. The tradeoff between communication rate, synchronization accuracy, and sensing precision is analytically characterized. The adaptive control problem is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) and solved using a Deep Q-Network (DQN). The proposed approach incorporates physically consistent, observable metrics to enable intelligent closed-loop physical layer adaptation, demonstrating significant performance gains over fixed pilot schemes.

## 1 System Model
We consider a single-input single-output (SISO) Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM)-based Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC) system. The system operates in discrete time frames indexed by $t$. Each frame consists of $N$ OFDM symbols and $K$ subcarriers. The constant average transmit power per OFDM symbol is denoted by $P$. The system dynamically allocates a fraction of subcarriers for pilot transmission and distributes transmit power between pilot and data symbols.

### 1.1 OFDM Transmission Model
Let $\rho_t \in (0, 1)$ denote the pilot ratio at frame $t$. Then, the number of pilot subcarriers is:

$$|\mathcal{P}_t| = \rho_t K \quad (1)$$

The remaining $(1 - \rho_t)K$ subcarriers are used for data transmission.
The transmitted OFDM symbol at subcarrier $k$ and OFDM symbol index $n$ is given by:

$$X_t[k,n] = \begin{cases} \sqrt{\dfrac{\beta_t P}{\rho_t K}}\, s_p[k,n], & k \in \mathcal{P}_t \\ \\ \sqrt{\dfrac{(1-\beta_t)P}{(1-\rho_t)K}}\, s_d[k,n], & k \in \mathcal{D}_t \end{cases} \quad (2)$$

where:
* $\beta_t \in (0, 1)$ is the fraction of total power allocated to pilots,
* $s_p[k, n]$ and $s_d[k, n]$ are unit power pilot and data symbols respectively,
* $\mathcal{P}_t$ and $\mathcal{D}_t$ denote pilot and data index sets.

**Explanation:**
The parameter $\rho_t$ determines how many subcarriers are dedicated to synchronization and sensing. The parameter $\beta_t$ controls the fraction of total transmit power devoted to pilot signals. By scaling pilot symbols with $\sqrt{\dfrac{\beta_t P}{\rho_t K}}$, the total pilot power equals $\beta_t P$. The data subcarriers receive total power $(1 - \beta_t)P$. This structure ensures that the total transmit power constraint is satisfied at every frame. $\rho_t$ and $\beta_t$ are treated as adaptive control variables.

### 1.2 Channel and Impairment Model
The received signal at subcarrier $k$, symbol $n$, and frame $t$ is modeled as:

$$Y_t[k,n] = H_t X_t[k,n] e^{j 2\pi \epsilon_t n T_s} + W_t[k,n] \quad (3)$$

where:
* $H_t$ is the block-fading channel frequency response, assumed flat across subcarriers and constant over the frame duration,
* $\epsilon_t$ is the physical carrier frequency offset (CFO) measured in Hertz,
* $T_s$ is the OFDM symbol duration,
* $W_t[k, n] \sim \mathcal{CN}(0, \sigma^2)$ is additive white Gaussian noise.

**Explanation:**
For mathematical tractability, we assume a block-fading channel where the channel response $H_t$ is approximately flat across subcarriers and time-invariant over the frame duration. The exponential term represents the phase rotation induced by a physical CFO across OFDM symbols. Inter-carrier interference (ICI) generated by the CFO will be accounted for later as an equivalent additive noise term. 

### 1.3 CFO Dynamics
To ensure bounded state evolution for stable reinforcement learning, we model the CFO evolution as a mean-reverting autoregressive process:

$$\epsilon_{t+1} = \alpha_c \epsilon_t + \nu_t, \quad |\alpha_c| < 1 \quad (4)$$

$$\nu_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_\nu^2) \quad (5)$$

**Explanation:**
A purely unbounded random walk can lead to unbounded synchronization error and unstable learning. The parameter $\alpha_c$ ensures the CFO remains within a finite range in the steady state, mimicking physical oscillator bounds, while the innovation term $\nu_t$ captures hardware instability and drift between frames.

## 2 Synchronization and Sensing Estimation
### 2.1 CFO Estimation and Implicit Assumptions
To estimate the CFO, we utilize pilot subcarriers. A reliable least-squares estimator evaluates the phase difference between consecutive OFDM symbols. This relies on the following structural assumptions:
1. **Pilot Repetition:** Pilot symbols remain identical across consecutive OFDM symbols: $X_t[k, n+1] = X_t[k, n], \forall k \in \mathcal{P}_t$.
2. **Quasi-Static Channel:** The channel is constant across adjacent symbols: $H_t[k, n+1] \approx H_t[k, n]$.
3. **Moderate-to-High SNR:** Noise-induced phase perturbations do not dominate the angle operation.

Under these conditions, the ratio simplifies such that $\dfrac{Y_t[k, n+1]}{Y_t[k, n]} \approx e^{j2\pi\epsilon_t T_s}$, allowing us to extract the CFO via:

$$\hat{\epsilon}_t = \dfrac{1}{2\pi T_s}\cdot \dfrac{1}{|\mathcal{P}_t|(N-1)}\sum_{k\in \mathcal{P}_t}\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\angle\!\left(\dfrac{Y_t[k,n+1]}{Y_t[k,n]}\right) \quad (6)$$

**Explanation:**
Because there are $N-1$ consecutive pairs in a frame of $N$ symbols, the normalization properly averages over $|\mathcal{P}_t|(N-1)$ observations to yield an unbiased estimate. 

### 2.2 Observable Synchronization Error Metric
The true residual error $\tilde{\epsilon}_t = \epsilon_t - \hat{\epsilon}_t$ is fundamentally unobservable at the receiver, making it invalid for use as an online state or reward variable. To evaluate synchronization performance in a physically realizable manner, we define a measurable surrogate metric based on pilot-domain residual mismatch:

$$ \mathcal{E}_t = \dfrac{1}{|\mathcal{P}_t|(N-1)} \sum_{k \in \mathcal{P}_t} \sum_{n=1}^{N-1} \left| Y_t[k, n+1] - Y_t[k, n]e^{j2\pi\hat{\epsilon}_t T_s} \right|^2 \quad (7) $$

This metric captures the residual phase inconsistency after CFO compensation and can be computed directly from received pilot symbols.

## 3 Communication Performance Analysis
### 3.1 Residual CFO and ICI Power
The inter-carrier interference (ICI) power caused by residual CFO can be approximated as:

$$\sigma^2_{\mathrm{ICI},t} \approx C \cdot \tilde{\epsilon}_t^2 P |H_t|^2 \quad (8)$$

where $C \approx \dfrac{\pi^2}{3 (\Delta f)^2}$ is a proportionality constant.

**Explanation:**
Residual CFO after estimation introduces imperfect orthogonality among subcarriers. The scaling by total transmit power $P$ and the channel gain $|H_t|^2$ is critical, as self-interference from all subcarriers propagates through the exact same fading channel as the desired signal.

### 3.2 Achievable Data Rate
The effective SINR at frame $t$ is:

$$\mathrm{SINR}_t = \dfrac{(1-\beta_t)P |H_t|^2}{\sigma^2 + \sigma^2_{\mathrm{ICI},t}} \quad (9)$$

The normalized spectral efficiency (bps/Hz) is:

$$R_t = (1-\rho_t)\log_2(1+\mathrm{SINR}_t) \quad (10)$$

## 4 Sensing Performance Analysis
### 4.1 Sensing SNR and Empirical Metric
Sensing resolution depends heavily on the effective pilot-domain signal-to-noise ratio, which must account for both pilot power and pilot density across the allocated subcarriers:

$$ \mathrm{SNR}_{\mathrm{pilot},t} = \dfrac{\beta_t P |H_t|^2}{\rho_t K \sigma^2} \quad (11) $$

Instead of using theoretical bounds (like the Cramér–Rao Lower Bound) which cannot be observed directly during online operation, we define an empirical sensing quality metric $S_t$. In practice, $S_t$ can be constructed as a proxy based on the sharpness of the delay correlation function or an empirically measured delay estimation error. 

## 5 Problem Formulation
We aim to design adaptive pilot ratio $\rho_t$ and pilot power fraction $\beta_t$ to maximize long-term system utility. To ensure strict observability, the instantaneous reward is defined purely using measurable metrics:

$$r_t = \lambda_1 R_t - \lambda_2 \mathcal{E}_t - \lambda_3 S_t \quad (12)$$

The objective is:

$$\max_{\{\rho_t,\beta_t\}} \mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^t r_t\right] \quad (13)$$

subject to constraints: $0 < \rho_t < 1$, and $0 < \beta_t < 1$.

## 6 Markov Decision Process Formulation
The problem is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) defined by the tuple $(\mathcal{S}, \mathcal{A}, \mathcal{P}, r, \gamma)$.

### 6.1 State Space Definition
To preserve strict causality, the state must be constructed using only information available *prior* to the current decision. The agent observes measurements from the previously completed frame $t-1$:

$$ s_t = \left( \hat{\epsilon}_{t-1}, \mathcal{E}_{t-1}, \overline{\mathrm{SINR}}_{t-1}, \overline{\mathrm{SNR}}_{\mathrm{pilot},t-1}, |\hat{H}_{t-1}|^2, \rho_{t-1}, \beta_{t-1} \right) \quad (14) $$

**Explanation:**
This formulation strictly uses delayed observations to prevent causality loops (i.e., observing the outcome of an action before choosing it). All state variables are measurable. The observable mismatch $\mathcal{E}_{t-1}$ informs the agent of current synchronization quality, while the delayed actions $\rho_{t-1}$ and $\beta_{t-1}$ help the agent map past decisions to current observations.

### 6.2 Action Space Definition
We discretize the control variables for DQN implementation:

$$\rho \in \{0.1,0.2,0.3,0.4\}, \quad \beta \in \{0.2,0.4,0.6\} \quad (15)$$

Thus, the total action space size is $|\mathcal{A}| = 12$. Discretization simplifies neural network output design and ensures stable learning.

### 6.3 State Transition Model
Channel evolution is modeled as block fading:

$$H_{t+1} = \alpha H_t + \sqrt{1-\alpha^2}\, z_t \quad (16)$$

where $z_t \sim \mathcal{CN}(0, 1)$ and $\alpha$ determines temporal correlation. Together with the bounded CFO process, the transitions satisfy the Markov property.

## 7 Deep Q-Network Solution
### 7.1 Action-Value Function
The optimal action-value function satisfies the Bellman optimality equation:

$$Q^{\ast}(s_t, a_t) = \mathbb{E} \left[ r_t + \gamma \max_{a'} Q^{\ast}(s_{t+1}, a') \mid s_t, a_t \right] \quad (17)$$

### 7.2 Neural Network Approximation
We approximate $Q(s, a)$ using a neural network parameterized by $\theta$:

$$L(\theta) = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\left(r_t + \gamma \max_{a'} Q(s_{t+1},a';\theta^{-}) - Q(s_t,a_t;\theta)\right)^2\right] \quad (18)$$

where $\theta^{-}$ represents the target network parameters.

3 : Initialize target network with weights $\theta^{-} = \theta$<br>
4 : **for** each episode **do**<br>

6 : &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; **for** each frame $t$ **do**<br>

## 9 Discussion on Convergence and Stability

While standard DQN with nonlinear function approximation lacks formal mathematical convergence guarantees, the proposed framework ensures stable empirical performance through physically grounded modeling choices. The shift to a mean-reverting autoregressive process for the CFO guarantees a bounded physical environment. Coupled with a reward function derived entirely from observable and inherently bounded surrogate metrics ($\mathcal{E}_t$, $S_t$, $R_t$), the agent is protected from the unbounded gradient variances typically caused by raw theoretical error expressions. Along with standard mechanisms like experience replay and target networks, these structural constraints ensure that the DQN agent can reliably and stably track environmental drifts across consecutive frames.
